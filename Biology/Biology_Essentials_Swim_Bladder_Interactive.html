How does a fish control its buoyancy?

Biology Essentials · A1.1.6 — Swim bladder and displacement of water

1. Adjust the swim bladder

A typical bony fish can regulate the amount of gas in its swim bladder. This changes its volume and the water it displaces, without greatly changing its mass.

About the model: This is an illustrative gas-bladder fish, not a particular species. The fish's fixed tissue and body components have mass 1.05 kg and displace 0.95 L; the gas adds volume with negligible mass. Freshwater density is 1.00 kg/L. At the selected depth, gas volume is estimated using Boyle's law, with surface pressure 1 atmosphere and an additional atmosphere for every 10 m of depth. The gas-volume slider sets the volume the bladder would have at the surface; fish in nature actively regulate bladder gas, and changes are not necessarily instantaneous. This is a conceptual model, not a prediction of actual swimming or diving behaviour.
